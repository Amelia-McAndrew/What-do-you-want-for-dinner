# What do you want for dinner?

A simple dashboard for keeping track of the food you have at home.

- **Today dashboard** – the app opens on Today, with four cards: today's meals (the next one highlighted by time of day; after 9pm it shows tomorrow), day by day (pick any of the next 7 days, or move a week at a time), going off (use-by food that's gone off or going off in 3 days, with Used it / Bin it, plus anything running low), and your shopping list. Tap a dish to see its recipe while cooking.
- **Shopping list** – a Shop tab (also shown on Today) that the household adds to: type anything (several at once with commas), tick things off as you shop, then **Put ticked in Food** (milk goes in the fridge, pasta in the cupboard and so on) or **Clear ticked**. Items are grouped by aisle type, show who added them and which meal they're for, and can be copied as text. **Fill from meal plan** on the Shop tab lets you pick dates (or Next 7 days / This week / Next week), previews what the planned meals in that range need, and adds it all in one go. Each ingredient appears once with the total for the period (amounts like 200g + 1kg, 2 tbsp + 1 tsp or tins are added up), minus what's already in Food when the units match; refilling updates the amounts instead of adding repeats, and things you typed in yourself are left alone.
- **Missing ingredients prompt** – when you add a meal to the plan, any ingredients that aren't in stock or already on the list come up with a choice each: add to the shopping list, "I have it" (adds it to Food in the place you pick, so it counts as in stock) or skip.
- **Storage places** – starts with Fridge, Freezer and Cupboard; add, rename or delete your own (e.g. "Garage freezer", "Spice rack").
- **Food items** – for each item record which storage place it's in, when it goes off, how much is left (with a unit), and optionally the full amount so you get a "how much is left" bar.
- **Food types** – each item has a type (Fruit & veg, Meat & fish, Dairy & eggs, Bread & bakery, Pasta, rice & grains, Tins & jars, Sauces, oils & spices, Baking, Snacks & sweets, Drinks, Other). It's filled in from the name as you type and can be changed; older items get a guessed type. Filter the food list by type, and the meal plan's shopping list is grouped by type.
- **Use by or best before** – each date is marked as use by or best before. Use-by dates drive the warnings; best-before dates are only about quality, so they never count as going off and just show a grey "Best before" label. New items default from their type (meat, fish and dairy are use by, everything else best before); items saved earlier count as use by.
- **Dashboard** – totals for items, things going off in the next 3 days, things that have gone off, and things running low.
- **Use these first** – a list of food that's about to go off, so you know what to cook tonight.
- **Fill in from Excel** – on the Food tab, tap **📊 Excel** to download a blank template (`food-template.xlsx`) with columns for the food, where it's kept, how much is left, unit, full amount, going-off date, use by / best before, type and notes, plus dropdowns for your storage places, types and units and a How to sheet. Fill it in with Excel, Google Sheets or Numbers and upload it to add everything at once (a .csv works too). Only the Food column is needed; new place names become new storage places, blanks are filled in as usual, dates like 25/10/2026 are read the UK way, and food already in the same place is updated rather than added twice. You see a summary before anything is added. The reader and writer are built in (`spreadsheet.js`), so no library or account is needed.
- Search, sort (soonest to go off, name, least left), a quick "−1" button to use some up, and export/import of your data as JSON.
- **Recipes tab** – store recipes with their ingredients (one per line), an optional method, a difficulty level (easy / medium / hard) and the total time. Each recipe is either a full meal or one part of a meal (main, side, sauce or dip, starter, dessert, drink), and you can filter by kind. Each recipe shows how many of its ingredients you have in stock, and you can sort by most ingredients in stock, quickest or easiest, or filter by difficulty.
- **Ready-made food** – tap **📦 + Add ready-made** on the Recipes tab for things you buy and just cook or serve (oven chips, garlic bread, coleslaw, fish fingers, Yorkshire puddings…). Just a name, cooking time and optional cooking instructions; common ones are suggested, and the kind is picked from the name (a side by default, so it's part of a meal, or a dessert, sauce, main or full meal where that fits). In the meal plan they have their own 📦 Ready-made group, so after picking a main you can add chips alongside it. They show In stock / Not in stock, go through the missing-ingredients prompt and onto the shopping list like anything else, and can be filtered and shared.
- **Recipe categories** – tag recipes with any number of categories: cuisine (Asian, Italian, Mexican, Indian, British, Mediterranean, American, Middle Eastern, French), season (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter, Christmas), diet (Vegetarian, Vegan, Healthy, Gluten free) and occasion (Quick, Comfort food, Fakeaway, Batch cook, Slow cooker, BBQ, Sunday roast, Kid friendly, Hosting), or add your own. Recipes for the current season show **In season**, and the Recipes tab can filter by category, including **In season now**. Copy for Claude asks for categories too. **✨ Suggest categories** in the recipe form and **✨ Auto-categorise** on the Recipes tab pick categories from the name, ingredients, method and time using built-in rules (free and offline); Auto-categorise only fills in recipes that don't have any yet.
- **Share recipes** – tap ↗ on a recipe (or **Share** when viewing one) to get a link and a QR code. The recipe travels inside the link, compressed, so anyone can open it and tap **Save to my recipes** – no account, household or server needed. Very long recipes skip the QR code and use the link only.
- **Recipes from Pinterest / TikTok** – save a recipe's link (it shows an "Open on Pinterest / TikTok" button), paste the caption or recipe text to fill in the ingredients and method automatically, or on Android share a pin or video straight to the installed app (**Share → Dinner**).
- **Copy for Claude** – in a new recipe, tap **✨ Copy for Claude** to copy a ready-made request (with the link and caption) to paste into the Claude app. Claude writes the recipe in a layout the app understands; paste the reply back and tap **Fill in from text** to fill in the name, difficulty, time, ingredients and method. Free with any Claude account, no API key.
- **Meal plan tab** – plan breakfast, lunch and dinner for each day of the week (Monday to Sunday) by picking from your saved recipes. A meal can be one full-meal recipe or several parts together, like a main, sides and a sauce. Move between weeks, clear a week, and get a shopping list of the ingredients you don't already have, which you can copy.
- **Find a meal** – at the top of the Plan tab, filter your recipes by ingredients (e.g. "chicken, rice" finds recipes with both), themes (categories, including In season), kind and "mostly in stock", then add any of them to a day and meal. **🎡 Spin the wheel** puts the matching recipes (up to 16) on a wheel that picks one at random, ready to add to the plan.
- **Households** – tap your picture in the top-left corner, then **Create or join a household**, and send an invite link; everyone who joins shares the same food list, recipes and meal plan, kept in sync between phones (and still working offline). Uses a free Firebase project – see below.
- **Household owner** – whoever creates a household is its owner (households made before owners existed can be claimed once with **Make me the owner**). The panel lists every phone in the household. The owner can tap **New code** to make a fresh join code (old invite links stop working, but phones already in stay in), **Remove** a phone that shouldn't be there (this also changes the code, so it can't rejoin with the old one), or **Make owner** to hand over. Only the owner can add or edit other people's profiles; everyone else can only add and edit themselves. The owner can't leave while other phones are still in the household.
- **You and your household** – your picture sits in the top-left corner (with a green dot when you're sharing). Tapping it slides out a panel with your profile (Edit me, Switch person), your household's sync status, code, invite link and Leave, everyone in it (with how much food and how many recipes each person has added), totals for food, recipes, meals planned this week and storage places, and Export / Import data. It works the same on phones and computers.
- **Profiles** – in the panel from your picture, pick who you are or add a person with a name and photo. There are 24 built-in characters to choose from, 12 foods and 12 animals (`avatars.js`), and new people get a random one. Food and recipes show the picture of whoever added them. Photos are shrunk to a small square on the phone and stored with the household's data, so no paid storage is needed.

## Running it

There's no build step. Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
# then visit http://localhost:8000
```

## Installing it on your phone

The app is an installable web app (PWA): once installed it opens full screen from your home screen, works offline, and saves your food list on the phone.

1. Host it with GitHub Pages: in the repo go to **Settings → Pages**, choose **Deploy from a branch**, pick the branch and `/ (root)`, and save. After a minute you get a link like `https://<username>.github.io/What-do-you-want-for-dinner/`.
2. Open that link on your phone:
   - **iPhone (Safari):** tap the Share button, then **Add to Home Screen**.
   - **Android (Chrome):** tap the ⋮ menu, then **Install app** (or **Add to Home screen**).

Data is saved on the device in the browser's local storage, so each phone or computer keeps its own list. Use **Export data** / **Import data** to back it up or move it to another device. Deleting the app from your home screen also deletes its saved data, so export a backup first.

## Setting up household sharing

Household sharing stores data in a Firebase project on Google's free Spark plan. The app works without it; until it's set up, the household section says sharing isn't switched on.

1. In the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com), create a project (Google Analytics not needed).
2. Add a **Web app** and copy its config object into `firebase-config.js` as `window.FIREBASE_CONFIG = { ... }`.
3. **Build → Authentication → Get started → Anonymous → Enable.**
4. **Build → Firestore Database → Create database** (production mode, a location near you), then paste the contents of `firestore.rules` into the **Rules** tab and **Publish**.

Each household lives at `households/<id>` (holding its `owner` and current `code`) with `places`, `items`, `recipes`, `plan`, `members` and `shopping` subcollections (one document per entry), so edits from different phones merge instead of overwriting each other. Each phone that joins gets a record in `households/<id>/devices`, and the rules only let registered phones read or write the household; a phone can only register with the current code, which it finds through `invites/<code>`. Households and invites can't be listed. Older households (whose id is their code) keep working, and their phones register automatically when they next open the app. The Firebase SDK (12.19.0, Apache 2.0) is bundled in `vendor/firebase.mjs`.
